Before you begin
  • There is no single right version of a movement; choose the option that feels steady and comfortable today.
  • Use a stable, non-wheeled chair for seated variations, with both feet supported where possible.
  • Move slowly, breathe normally and pause whenever you need to.
  • Consult a healthcare professional before starting; this guide is general information, not individualized medical advice.
A welcoming place to begin

Movement can meet you where you are

Starting at home can make movement feel more private, flexible and familiar. You do not need a special room or equipment. A little clear floor space, a sturdy chair and permission to go at your own pace are enough to explore these ideas.

People bring different bodies, mobility, energy, experience and health needs to movement. The aim here is not to keep up with someone else or to make every exercise look a certain way. It is to find a version that feels manageable for you, and to stop or adapt whenever your body asks for a change.

Each movement below includes a standing option and a chair-based alternative. Pick one version, use a smaller range, or simply practise the setup. Any of those can be a useful starting point.

Comfort before challenge

Set up a space that feels supportive

Choose a steady seat

Use a firm chair that does not roll, set on a level surface. If possible, place it near a wall for extra stability.

Make room to move

Clear nearby clutter and wear whatever feels comfortable and secure for your space. Keep water within reach if you want it.

Let comfort guide you

Keep your breath easy and movements controlled. Pause for rest; stop if you feel pain, dizziness, chest discomfort or unusual shortness of breath.

Please check in with a professional

Consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new exercise routine, particularly if you have a health condition, injury, balance concern or questions about what is appropriate for you. Follow their personal guidance.

Eight gentle ideas, two ways to explore

Choose a version that feels comfortable

Try just one or two movements at first. Keep the range small and the pace unhurried. Repetitions are suggestions, not a target; stop sooner or rest whenever you need to.

01

Seated march

From a stable chair, sit tall and lift one foot a little, then set it down and switch sides. Keep the movement small and relaxed.

Standing option Hold the chair back or a stable counter and gently shift your weight from one foot to the other, lifting a heel if comfortable.

02

Shoulder rolls

Sit comfortably and slowly circle your shoulders up, back and down. Let your arms rest by your sides.

Standing option Stand with a comfortable stance and make the same small, easy circles. Keep your neck relaxed.

03

Seated arm reaches

Reach one hand gently forward or slightly upward, only as far as feels easy, then lower it and change sides.

Standing option Stand near support and alternate relaxed forward reaches. There is no need to reach overhead.

04

Heel and toe lifts

With both feet supported, lift your heels while keeping the balls of your feet down, then lower. Next, lift the toes while keeping heels down.

Standing option Hold a stable support and gently lift heels, then toes, using a small range. Skip either part if it feels unsteady.

05

Seated side reaches

Sit tall, let one hand slide a little down the outside of your thigh, then return to center. Switch sides without forcing a stretch.

Standing option Stand with support close by and slide one hand gently toward the same-side thigh. Keep both feet grounded.

06

Seated leg straightening

From the chair, slowly straighten one knee only as far as comfortable, pause briefly, and lower the foot. Change sides.

Standing option With both hands near a stable support, slide one foot a short distance forward and return it. Keep the knee soft.

07

Gentle chest opening

Sit with hands resting on your thighs. Let your shoulders soften back and your chest feel open, then return to a relaxed position.

Standing option Stand comfortably and draw your shoulders gently back, keeping your arms low. Avoid arching your back.

08

Supported sit-to-stand

If it feels suitable, sit toward the front of a sturdy chair, lean forward slightly and press through your feet to rise. Use your hands for support as needed.

Chair option Stay seated and practise leaning forward a little, then sitting tall again. You can also gently press your feet into the floor without standing.

It is completely fine to choose the chair variation for every movement. Comfort and steadiness matter more than how an exercise looks.

A flexible way to build a rhythm

Let four weeks be a gentle framework

The Move at Home guide uses a four-week structure to help you return to movement regularly. Treat it as a menu of options: begin with a short, comfortable session, allow rest between active days and repeat a week whenever you want more time.

WEEK 01

Find your starting point

Explore a few movements and notice which versions feel comfortable. A short session is enough to begin.

WEEK 02

Choose a familiar rhythm

Return to movements you already know. Keep the same length or make a small adjustment only if it feels right.

WEEK 03

Practise coming back

Keep the routine flexible. Use the chair options, take breaks and let a shorter session count.

WEEK 04

Keep what works

Notice which movements fit your space and day. Repeat, adapt or rest according to your needs.

A missed day does not need making up

Return when you are ready, with the next small step that suits you. Rest is part of a flexible routine, not a setback to repay.

Make it fit real life

Small adjustments are part of the practice

  • Choose one movement. You do not need to complete all eight in one sitting.
  • Use a smaller range. A small, comfortable motion is a valid variation.
  • Take pauses. Rest between movements or stop for the day whenever needed.
  • Repeat a week. There is no deadline to progress through the four-week structure.
  • Ask for personal guidance. A qualified professional can help you consider adaptations for your needs.
A kind next step

Let movement belong to you

You can start with one seated march, a shoulder roll or a few comfortable breaths before deciding what comes next. There is no need to prove anything or turn a gentle practice into a test.

Choose the version that supports you today. If today is a day for rest, you can return another time and begin again with care.

No shortcuts. No miracles. Real progress, one habit at a time.
